Chapter 1.

The public library had too many ears. It was not exactly quiet, with its whispered orchestra of slow footsteps and keyboard typing, but it was also not quiet enough for two people to discuss the end of their relationship. There was a persistent feeling hanging mid-air, as if someone would at any time walk through them as they ambiguously looked at each other in the middle of the long otherwise empty hallway spaced between each bookshelf. French literature. Portuguese literature. They looked at each other, each of the two painted over a canvas of plum red and olive green leather covers and dusty cursive vertical titles and light grey metal dividers. He was always taller than she was - height is not something one can change - but for some reason not today. In the present moment they were eye to eye, his blue iris and her brown iris. His eyes were big and at the very edge of tears, like lakes. One man and one woman looking at each other on a rainy day, on one of the very first days of spring where the air tastes like the objects in a room. Like an apple peel, like a carpet, like a wool coat. Everything emanated a slight scent. Standing in between the world literature section on the second floor of the public library, they spoke in half sentences. One would glance at the left to see if that was the sound of someone coming their way, one would look at their hands as if they were pages of a dictionary to consult for the exact word wanted to convey. It seemed rehearsed, like a contemporary dance choreography. They were also very beautiful, which gave it all the appearance of a still life painting. Like a bowl of fruit and insects or like a true theatre costume fitting, the light above them warm and dramatic. He looked very polished, like an office worker. Dark blue tweed jacket, brown briefcase. Underneath, a sliver of a cotton off-white dress shirt folding into his belt and trousers. Black leather shoes, a bit too pointed. He looked geometrical, his hair parted on the side and his gold-rimmed aviator eyeglasses balanced on a long nose. She was beautiful too, but in a different and sweeter way. One long braid untied, held together by will or memory of hair. Canadian tuxedo, full raw unwashed denim on denim. A polka dot red and white shoulder bag, held in between both of her hands in front of her buttoned up chest. Orange patent leather flats with a small bow. Every small detail, her straight posture and the crossing of her feet and the slight leaning back on the book covers; it all seemed as if she was running an errand, simply about to go elsewhere and not really bothered if someone interrupted but acting as if simply out of kindness.

“You said - you said I could remind you to call me or see me or plan things. I don’t want to remind you to love me. It’s not something I want to do for myself. There is no air here. I don’t think it’s raining anymore, maybe we can go on a walk.”

The pair walked in a line, her before him, towards one end of the bookshelf corridor.

His eyes were big, step after step leather shoes in mud. The park was one large pond of water and one table with benches on the centre. It was a quiet park near the university, in front of one of the classics inspired buildings. A row of towering columns of grey marble, and a current of students and teachers cutting through the park to go from one class to another. After the rainstorm, the flow of people was absent. The only voices rippling through the air were of the couple. A panorama shot into the scene, a wide angle lens and all one can see is an oval of olive tinted grass and polka dots of water. A couple of tall trees at the corners of the park, right before the fence facing the asphalt street. In the oval, there was a very sweet silence. It was almost as if all sound stayed in it untouched by the moving cars on one side or the threads of passengers on the other side. On sunny days couples and friends loved to sit at the feet of the trees, reading to each other or laughing or doing nothing. No regular benches, aside from the slightly off centre wooden table with a long bench on both sides. The rainstorm had left everyone asleep, the city was scared of ruining its attire and every bird was playing cards on the twigs above the clouds. It was quiet. The couple was swimming in the oval moving park, a sea of brown grass. Closer. Closer. Her lips were moving fast, lipstick drawn in a coral colour. The man was crying and one big tear falls on the wood, one as big as the puddle touching his trousers under the table. She looked at him with rising eyebrows, like one does with a child. Maybe she was waiting for him to stop crying and say something significant, maybe she was displeased at his crying or maybe even secretly flattered at his sorrow. There was a microscopic smile behind each word, hiding like a flashing light. Closer. Closer. One in front of the other, again. The girl - “You will find better. Smarter and more beautiful and kinder. You were funny and so smart and nice but so lazy. I don’t want to be loved like that. It’s not that it wasn’t love, but I want a different kind.” His hands held together, he stayed still shaking his head with a sigh. Every sentence she said, she said twice. Just to fill the time hanging and the space in between. Closer. Their faces were both pale, a little humid. An ant on her shoulder. His glasses somewhat wet but not fully yet. The scene goes silent, her lips are still moving and his hands still together. Lipreading, every word is a double. Now, they both laugh. She does first, then he looks up at her and smiles. The image oscillates, it’s grainy.
